# Writing for slides

A slide title states what the slide shows or what the audience must decide. The body explains that point. Use the terms the audience needs for the subject; explain an unfamiliar term when it first affects the decision.

Read all titles in order, then read each slide at its delivery size. Replace abstract instructions with the action supported by the slide. A reconciliation test slide should name the conditions to test and the expected result. Keep prices, estimates, exclusions and approval conditions exactly.

A subtitle earns its place by adding an explanation or condition. Remove it when it repeats the title. Diagram labels can name a system, field or state without becoming full sentences. Body copy should explain relationships in natural sentences; avoid fragment stacks and repeated card formulas.

Review every diagram label and table cell. Follow the direction of each arrow, read each branch condition and check that the words match the proposed behavior. Do not replace technical detail with a vague claim about confidence or readiness.

Run the writing checker and reconcile figures against their sources. Keep its settings unchanged. Then inspect every rendered slide for readable text and complete content. If the copy does not fit, revise the layout or wording without losing the condition or figure the reader needs.
