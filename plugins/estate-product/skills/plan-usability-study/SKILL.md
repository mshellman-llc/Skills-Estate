# Plan usability study

Choose a research question that can change a product decision. Use the supplied product context and existing research before requesting information already available. A missing participant roster does not prevent a useful test plan.

For planning, read [Session design](references/session-design.md). Select the audience and product state, then write scenarios around participants' goals. Keep navigation labels and intended click paths out of task wording. Define observable completion conditions separately for the moderator.

Use a sample suited to the question and distinct user groups. A small formative study can reveal problems; it cannot establish population rates or prove that most problems have been found. Record the recruitment rationale and the groups not represented.

For findings, read the complete supplied observations. Keep participant reports, directly observed behavior, and interpretation distinct. Link each finding to its task and source. Count observed opportunities as well as failures. Preserve a severe problem seen by one participant even when it is uncommon in the sample.

Agent walkthroughs and static inspections can identify issues to investigate. Label their evidence accurately. Do not invent participants, quotations, timestamps, recordings, success rates, or research validation. With no session evidence, return a plan or provisional inspection findings and the evidence needed to evaluate them.

Use participant codes and omit unnecessary identifying details. Record consent requirements before recording or recruitment; a plan does not authorize contacting people or uploading recordings. Avoid real purchases or other consequential actions in test tasks unless already authorized.

Deliver the requested plan or findings with the decision each finding informs. Recommend a repair and a focused retest when supported. A recommendation is not a verified fix.

This skill adds research methods to the existing setup. Its source authority, voice, brand, and completion rules remain with the governing instructions and their existing skills.
