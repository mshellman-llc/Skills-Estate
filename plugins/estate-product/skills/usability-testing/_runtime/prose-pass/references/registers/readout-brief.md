# Assessment readouts and executive briefs

Open with the supported finding or decision. Put methodology where the reader
needs it to assess the conclusion. Preserve detailed technical evidence in the
document or a linked appendix when it affects that decision.

## Explain a finding

Give the observed condition and its source. Explain a consequence when the
evidence supports the relationship. Recommend the concrete next action where
one follows. These checks do not require an identical paragraph for every
finding or a number in a claim that has no measured quantity.

A count of automation rules does not establish performance causation. Keep
observations separate from a proposed diagnosis. Avoid inventing a financial
loss or implying negligence to make an assessment sound more consequential.

Prioritize findings by their supported effect on the requested decision.
Do not manufacture equal praise and criticism or append a general promise
that every problem will produce an opportunity.

## Match the reader

Use the confirmed author's voice. An executive needs the decision and its
supporting evidence early. An architect may need object-level detail. Keep
the terminology that explains the actual behavior in either version.

Compare figures with their source notes. Review a reported observation at
the strength the source supports. A shorter sentence cannot make it true.

## Worked example

This example uses fictional assessment observations. A log shows that quote
Q-17 entered approval on Monday and received a decision on Friday. No other
quote history is available.

A suitable finding is: “Quote Q-17 waited from Monday to Friday for an approval
decision. Check whether other quotes show the same delay before changing the
approval process.”

The finding reports the observed interval. It does not invent an average or
identify approval as the largest bottleneck without comparative evidence.
