# Contract and proposal writing

Use this register for a proposal or contract. Read the evaluator's criteria or
the selected contract profile before editing. Preserve commitments and the
source of every commercial figure.

## Explain the specific work

Name the client's supported problem and the proposed behavior. Replacing a
client name in a generic paragraph does not explain its scope. Retain the
actual systems and volume assumptions relevant to the agreement. Let the
content determine paragraph length; do not force identical section shapes.

Remove repeated clause explanations. Keep each obligation in its governing
section and cross-reference it where necessary. Use the specialist contract
review module for duplicated obligations and inconsistent definitions.

## Preserve the commitment

Check whether a rewrite strengthens a capability or implies staffing the
provider has not confirmed. Keep material uncertainty in the claim. Writing
review cannot change a price or authorize a scope change.

Use the confirmed author's voice. Locate assumptions in the contract's
designated estimate section; do not presume an exhibit number or house legal
template. Explain supported business impact without inventing a loss figure.

## Reconcile the figures

Run the figure comparison against the authoritative quote or versioned
estimate. Check durations and resource counts too. Preserve required terms
and contractual punctuation. Read every change for altered legal meaning
after the mechanical checks pass.

## Worked example

This example uses fictional scope data. The input authorizes an automation
review capped at 10 hours. It includes a findings document and excludes code
changes. No performance measurement is available.

A suitable scope paragraph is: “The provider will review the Case automation
within a 10-hour cap and deliver its findings. The work excludes code changes.”

The paragraph preserves the cap and boundary. It makes no promise that the
review will establish the cause of a performance issue. If that diagnosis is
part of the requested scope, define its inputs and verification separately.
