# Voice

A catalog entry is published copy. It inherits the voice standard of wherever it is going. If the destination documents one, that standard governs and overrides everything below. Check for it before writing.

## The Estate default

- Zero em dashes anywhere, including headings. Use a comma, a colon, parentheses, or a full stop.
- Confident and direct. State the thing.
- No buzzwords, no filler hedging, no overstatement.

## Sentence shapes to strip

Vocabulary is the easy half. The stronger tell is the construction pattern, so read for these:

- **Antithesis flips**: "not just X, but Y". Say Y.
- **Trailing benefit clauses**: a comma followed by an "-ing" verb that explains the upside (", enabling teams to move faster"). Cut it or make it its own sentence.
- **Throat-clearing openers**: Ultimately, In short, Importantly, It's worth noting.
- **Rhetorical questions** used as transitions.
- **Vague "This means ..." openers** that restate the previous sentence.
- **"By doing X ..." wind-ups** that delay the point.
- **Rule-of-three triads** used for rhythm rather than because there are exactly three things.
- **Monotone runs**: five sentences of near-identical length. Vary them.

## The hard limit

A voice pass never changes what a line claims.

Leave untouched:

- Any figure, range, or band.
- Any boundary or exclusion.
- Any conclusion or recommendation.
- Epistemic language that carries real content: estimated, rough, approximately, a range, "open decision", "not yet grounded", "needs a human". This is not hedging. It is the honest state of the fact, and softening or deleting it turns an open question into a false promise.

If a voice edit would make a caveat read more confidently than the evidence supports, do not make it.

## Order of operations

Write for meaning first, then do the voice pass as a separate read. Trying to do both at once is how caveats get smoothed away.
