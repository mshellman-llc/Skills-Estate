# Register: client email, internal Slack, outreach

Short-form, high-frequency, and the register where perceived AI costs the most per word: 47% of B2B professionals say they'd be less likely to reply to a cold email they thought was AI-written, and what triggers the perception isn't provenance — it's formulaic structure, templated sentence rhythm, and generic non-personalization (Hunter.io 2025; recipients tested under 50% accurate at actually identifying AI). Managers who over-rely on AI for messages lose sincerity ratings, worst on relational/motivational messages (UF/USC 2025). Write like one specific person writing to one specific person.

## Client email

- One specific in the first sentence: their system, their number, their last conversation. "Following up on yesterday's discussion of the Case automation findings" beats "I hope this email finds you well."
- Concrete ask or next step at the end, with a date or an option. Not "let me know your thoughts."
- Kill chatbot closers: "Feel free to reach out," "Let me know if you need anything else," "I hope this helps." End where the content ends.
- No templated rhythm: three same-length sentences in a row reads as a sequence mail-merge. One fragment or one long sentence breaks it.
- Subject lines in sentence case, no colons-as-drama, no "Quick question."

## Internal Slack

- Casual, efficient, fragments fine, personality welcome. "FYI — updating the quick wins sheet, don't touch rows 4-12 until I'm done."
- Action and owner up front. Skip preamble entirely.
- This is the one register where staccato is natural — the deck rules do not apply here.

## Outreach / prospecting

- The audience-indictment hook ("What most RevOps teams get wrong...") is now explicitly targeted by platform slop classifiers and underperforms on engagement. Open with the prospect-specific observation instead: their stack, their posting, their event.
- One claim, one proof, one ask. Multi-benefit paragraphs read as generated.
- An imperfect, obviously human note outperforms polished template prose. Don't sand off the voice.

## Quick pass default

This register almost always takes the quick pass: scanner, fix hits, apply the rules above, return the rewrite with a one-line note. Reserve the 4-section audit for when someone asks for it.

## Worked example

Input: "We are excited to announce a transformative new initiative that leverages cutting-edge technology to streamline operations and empower our teams. The future looks bright as we embark on this journey together."

Rewrite: "We're launching a new initiative to simplify operations and give teams better tools. It uses the latest version of our internal platform, which cut processing time by 40% in pilot testing.

That's it. That's the update."

Why it works: the buzzwords became one concrete fact with a number; the closing fragment is natural in an internal register (and only there — don't transplant it into client deliverables).
