# Depth tests, the cardinal rule, and the clone test

Run these on the finished draft, after `case_gate.py` passes. The gate checks
structure; these check whether the case deserves to be believed. Report each
test's result, not an adjective.

## The depth tests

1. **Name real things.** Actual systems, pains, numbers, dates, people, every
   one sourced. No "the platform," no "significant savings."
2. **Lead with the recommendation.** The ask is stated up front and restated
   at the close, never buried. State why it wins; do not just assert it.
3. **Argue both sides honestly.** The case against carries the skeptic's real
   arguments at full strength, including the cheaper alternative. A case with
   no credible downside fails this test outright.
4. **Every benefit ties to a sourced pain.** An outcome that closes a named
   current-state break, not a feature. If the pain cannot be found in the
   ledger, the benefit gets cut.
5. **Every risk carries a severity and an action mitigation.** A mitigation
   that restates the risk in softer words fails.
6. **The cost of waiting is arithmetic.** Inputs, operator, result, printed
   on the page, every input sourced, tied to a real dated event.
7. **The ask is bounded.** What is priced, what is not, and the open
   decisions with owners and dates are all named. No blank checks.
8. **Timing is sourced.** "Why now" rests on a real event with a real date,
   not urgency theater.
9. **Costs are honest.** Where the investment is higher than the
   alternatives, the case says so and reframes it; it never hides it.
10. **Confidence renders honestly.** Confirmed as fact, Inferred as an
    inference with its basis and register ID, Unknown as a named gap,
    conflicts as flagged divergences with both values shown.

## The cardinal rule

Worked examples of this artifact, wherever the estate mounts them, are
depth-and-voice-and-density yardsticks consulted last, never content sources
and never structural molds. Everything in an example belongs to one specific
engagement. None of it appears in your case unless your ledger independently
sourced it for this deal.

Consult an example only after the structure is derived from the ledger, and
only to ask: is my recommendation this clear and this early? Is my case
against this honest? Does my cost-of-delay do arithmetic like this? Is my ask
this bounded? Evidence-core is explicit on the underlying rule: generated
artifacts and worked examples have zero evidentiary authority.

## The clone test

Run it before shipping. If find-and-replacing the client name would turn your
case back into a worked example or a prior deal's case, you have cloned it.
Every section must contain facts sourced from this deal's systems (its pains,
its risks, its number, its driving date) that could be true of no other deal.

A concrete tell: if your case carries a migration-cutover timing argument and
a SaaS-pricing rebuttal because the last case you saw carried them, and your
ledger holds neither, you reached for a shape instead of the ledger. Delete
and rebuild from the ledger.

## The judgment forks

Every real case contains at least one debatable call. Find yours, name it to
the user, and argue the other side honestly. The recurring ones:

- Whether the timing argument is strong enough to lead the executive frame,
  or whether the economics should.
- Whether an inferred value figure is safe to print in front of an approver,
  or belongs qualitative with the inference flagged.
- Which alternative the case against takes on first: the named competitor or
  do-nothing.
- Whether a conceded downside is survivable in this room, or the concession
  needs the sponsor briefed before the document circulates.

Presenting the case as settled when it holds a real fork is the failure mode.
The seller and sponsor know things about the room that the ledger does not.
