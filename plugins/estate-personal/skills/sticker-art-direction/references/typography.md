# Typography for short printed phrases

Use this reference to select and refine typography for stickers and other small printed objects. The workflow recommendations below apply published typography guidance to expressive short copy. They require visual judgment; they are not measurements of humor, originality, or commercial appeal.

## Start with the reading

Preserve the supplied text in a copy record. Record proposed punctuation or wording changes separately. Read the phrase aloud and identify its delivery: an interruption, a direct statement, a reversal, a whispered aside, or another specific mechanism. Mark the relationships the reader needs to understand immediately.

For the synthetic phrase “This is not a rehearsal,” a dominant “REHEARSAL” beside an easily missed “not” could distort the first reading. Keep the negation perceptible within the composition. This does not require one prescribed arrangement: equal emphasis, deliberate contrast, or a continuous line may each work.

Choose line breaks for meaning and rhythm before fitting the text to a shape. Short phrases can work well centered; sensible line starts still require attention. Centering is an option, not the default for every phrase. [Matthew Butterick, Centered text](https://practicaltypography.com/centered-text.html)

## Audition type in the phrase

Compare complete phrases rather than alphabet specimens alone. Inspect the actual punctuation, repeated letters, word shapes, and distinctive glyphs that the design will use. Describe what each candidate contributes to the intended delivery. A supposed mood such as “friendly” remains a design hypothesis until the composition supports it.

Look beyond serif and sans-serif labels. Construction, stroke contrast, proportions, and finer details help explain why superficially similar faces behave differently. Define the role of a second face before adding it, then test its relationship with the first. Google Fonts' construction framework supports this process while explicitly allowing exceptions. [Google Fonts Knowledge, Pairing typefaces](https://github.com/google/fonts/blob/main/cc-by-sa/knowledge/modules/choosing_type/lessons/pairing_typefaces_based_on_their_construction_using_the_font_matrix/content.md)

Try variations within one family when weight, width, or italic can provide the needed distinction. Match apparent size and mass when that helps the composition; matching point sizes is not the objective. Hierarchy can use weight, style, size, and color without adding another family. [MIT EECS, Typography](https://web.mit.edu/6.813/www/sp18/classes/16-typography/)

## Refine space and density

Keep a proof using the font's original metrics. Distinguish overall tracking from pair-specific kerning. Adobe describes optical kerning as useful in particular circumstances, including limited font kerning data or mixed type sizes; it is not an automatic upgrade for every setting. [Adobe, About kerning and tracking](https://helpx.adobe.com/indesign/desktop/format-and-style-text/tabs-indents-and-spacing/about-kerning-and-tracking.html)

Judge whole words before correcting exceptional pairs. Inspect the white spaces between letters, then between words. Set line spacing around the actual capitals, descenders, and punctuation. A crowded composition can be intentional, but check whether letters merge or the sentence fragments. Excessive word or line gaps can also weaken phrase grouping. [MyFonts, Text Readability Manual](https://www.myfonts.com/a/font/content/the-font-manual/text-readability)

Preserve script connections and deliberate monospace rhythm. Automatic optical kerning can conflict with these features. Spacing also depends on size and viewing distance; no universal tracking value serves every context. [Peter Biľak, Size-Specific Spacing of Fonts](https://www.typotheque.com/articles/size-specific-spacing-of-fonts)

Check visible mass against the composition's edges. Punctuation and diagonal letters can appear indented despite mechanically aligned bounds. Adjust optical overhang where it improves the intended edge, keeping production safety margins separate. [Ilene Strizver, Hanging Punctuation](https://www.myfonts.com/pages/fontscom-learning-fontology-level-4-fine-typography-hanging-punctuation/)

## Compare meaningful alternatives

An alternative should change the reading experience through a different premise, structure, rhythm, or relationship among words. Font swaps inside an unchanged layout are useful auditions, but should be labeled accordingly. Explain each candidate's intended effect without relying on its font name.

Evaluate where the eye lands and what it reads next. Check that contrast supports the intended emphasis and that density belongs to the phrase's delivery. Compare in black and white when color obscures weak structure; keep color in early tests when it carries the concept. Fix an unclear premise before polishing individual pairs.

## Review at output size

Inspect enlarged details, then return to proposed physical dimensions. Check counters, fine strokes, reverse text, and the smallest words on an actual-size print when possible. Optical cuts adapt letterforms for different use ranges; a display cut reduced to a small sticker may fail even when its enlarged proof looks attractive. Recommended ranges allow exceptions. [Type Network, Optical sizes](https://typenetwork.com/articles/inside-the-fonts-optical-sizes)

Record the selected treatment, font version, spacing adjustments, and reason for the choice. Retain editable or reconstructable type before outlining. Overflow checks and successful rendering support verification; approval of the reading and a suitable print proof remain separate decisions.
