# Continuation and change impact

## One home for each product

The home is the current entry point, not a container that must absorb every file. It routes to authoritative product records and code. Source evidence and completed artifacts remain linked there. Keep unresolved work beside the current checkpoint. Preserve the existing index and registers; add missing fields or links in their current format. Do not replace an established issue tracker or task authority.

The home needs these contents at each meaningful boundary:

- The requested outcome and stage, with a finish line. Identify source scope and authorization/protected-work boundary.
- Authority and artifact map, with stable IDs, locations, versions, state, and evidence links.
- Stage applicability and readiness gates. Identify blockers and stale evidence for each gate.
- Completed artifacts, with the boundary they satisfy and the receipt that verifies them.
- Open work, with affected records, dependencies, known owner/decider or an explicit unknown, and the evidence needed to resolve it.
- Latest checkpoint and exact next action. Name its inputs and target; record the completion receipt, authorization state, and stop condition.

Update the home after a completed source area, a material decision, a design revision, a build/acceptance result, a discovered blocker, or a session boundary. Preserve earlier checkpoints and decisions. An index link is enough when an existing artifact contains the substance.

## Continuation checkpoint

Use the checkpoint template in [the record bank](../assets/record-templates.json). Bind the checkpoint to:

1. The requested scope, applicable instructions, source snapshot/revisions, current product decisions, and build/model/pattern versions.
2. Artifacts completed within that boundary. Record each artifact's state and receipt; retain coverage still open.
3. Findings and material contradictions. Keep rejected approaches with their failure reasons. Retain user corrections that affect future work.
4. Applicable gate assessments and failed checks. Identify stale records; retain open decisions and blocked dependencies.
5. The exact next executable action and running processes/worktrees started for that action, if any.

On resume, read the home and latest checkpoint. Compare only the inputs needed for the requested next action. A dated receipt can remain evidence of a past result while no longer satisfying current readiness. Preserve a valid completed artifact even when one dependent section requires reassessment.

If the previous session ended before saving a checkpoint, reconstruct the boundary from available receipts and current records. Label any missing evidence. Do not infer completion from idle chat state.

## Change-impact tracking

Record the changed input's old and new revision/hash, the initiating event, directly affected records, and transitive dependents. Use existing traceability links to walk `source/decision → capability → job/journey/value → release item → design/architecture/agent → acceptance → verification → published or hub projection` as applicable. Links express actual dependencies; they are not a requirement to connect every record to every category.

| Changed input | Evidence that needs reassessment |
| --- | --- |
| Source bytes, revision, authority, or inclusion boundary | Reassess substantive review and derived claims. Scope expansion can reopen coverage even when old hashes match. |
| Product decision or release horizon | Affected requirements, priorities, designs, architecture choices, and expected acceptance outcomes. |
| Code, configuration, dependency, model, prompt, or pattern version | Relevant runtime, rendered, agent, and integration checks; preserve unrelated receipts when independence is supported. |
| Environment, permissions, connector, deployment, or fixture | Checks whose preconditions changed and claims about those destinations. |
| Customer evidence or alternative comparison | Value, persona, differentiation, and priority hypotheses that relied on it. |

Mark affected records and gates stale or not assessed before reusing their claims. Preserve prior receipts and approved historical decisions. Record the re-review/retest action and its result; a changed-source flag alone does not repair downstream prose or behavior.

Resolve conflicts through the product's source and decision authorities. If a current decision supersedes historical intent, retain both records and the supersession link. If two current authorities disagree, keep the conflict open and seek the smallest material decision while continuing independent work.

These are operating instructions. They do not create a background scanner, enforce a gate in software, update a task system, or install a recurring job.
