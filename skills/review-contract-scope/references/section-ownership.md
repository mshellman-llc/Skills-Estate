# Section ownership and cross-reference discipline

Map the selected agreement before editing. An obligation may deliberately appear in more than one legally significant place; do not remove it merely to reduce repetition. The goal is a clear controlling statement and consistent references without changing contractual effect.

## Build the actual ownership map

| Mechanic | Identify from the supplied agreement | Review |
|---|---|---|
| Estimate or commitment | Agreed baseline schedule and quantity status | Preserve caps, assumptions and billing model |
| Change process | Actual trigger, approval and amendment sections | Keep volume changes distinct from new workstreams where required |
| Delivery gates | Actual methodology or milestone schedule | Preserve owner, evidence and dependency |
| Acceptance | Agreed acceptance provisions | Preserve required wording and timelines |
| Integration and data scope | Scope, exclusions and attachments | Reconcile boundaries without broadening them |
| Access and delays | Responsibilities and agreed remedies | Do not invent remedies or shift ownership |

For each repeated passage, record all locations, the controlling source and whether the repetition is intentional. Confirm precedence and incorporation before selecting an owner. State authorized edits at that location, and replace other passages with accurate references only where permitted. Recheck every reference after renumbering.

## Synthetic illustration

Before: a sample review plan explains its file-validation procedure in both Overview and Validation. After: Overview says “Validation describes the file checks”; Validation retains the complete procedure. This invented editorial example has no contractual force and supplies no section numbers or terms for a real agreement.

Record retained repetitions with their reason. Report unresolved ownership or precedence conflicts rather than hiding them behind a cleaner sentence.
