# Register: assessment readouts, executive briefs, findings documents

The reader is a decision-maker who will skim. The document exists to transfer findings and force a decision, not to demonstrate effort.

## Structure rules

**Lead with the number or the decision.** The lede goes first: the figure, the count of findings, the recommendation. Backstory and methodology come after, if at all. A buried "$124,500 over 14 weeks" in the final paragraph is a structural AI tell as much as a writing one — AI drafts narrate chronologically (what we did, then what we found); executives read conclusion-first.

**State what you found, what it means, what to do.** Every finding gets three beats: the observation with its number, the consequence, the action. "44 active workflow rules on Case alone. That's the root cause of the performance issues your team flagged. Clean these up before any new build."

**No stakes inflation.** This register is where AI escalates a quoting delay into "a fundamental breakdown in the customer experience." The test is disproportionality: severity language with no anchoring number. Name the impact — deals lost, cycle time, margin leakage — or drop the severity claim. Findings carry their own weight when they're specific.

**No false balance.** "While challenges exist, the opportunities far outweigh them" takes no position. The readout's job is a position: which finding matters most, what to do first, what you'd defer. Sequence is a recommendation — say "the automation cleanup unblocks everything else," not "all three areas merit attention."

**No audience indictment.** Don't frame findings as the client's failure ("what your team has been getting wrong"). State conditions and causes. The client hired the assessment; they don't need to be positioned as negligent to justify it.

## Voice calibration

Direct findings, specific data points, "we" voice for actions taken and recommendations made. Executive variant: shorter, decision-first, light on spec. Architect variant: full object-level detail, preserve every technical term.

## Tie-out

Every figure in the readout came from somewhere — a metadata query, a log, an interview. `--tie-out` against the source notes; an assessment that misstates a count it measured loses the room.

## Worked example

Input: "It is worth noting that our analysis revealed pivotal insights regarding your approval processes, which serve as a significant blocker, potentially impacting your overall operational efficiency."

Rewrite: "Approvals are the biggest blocker. A quote waits 5-7 days on average for sign-off."

Why it works: copula restored ("are"), hedges cut, the claim anchored to the measured number, eleven words doing what twenty-six pretended to.
