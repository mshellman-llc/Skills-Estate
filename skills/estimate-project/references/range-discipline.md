# Estimate ranges

A range needs a source and an explanation of what moves its bounds. Use
the supplied work breakdown or relevant comparables. Explain a proposed
estimating choice before treating it as accepted scope.

## Choose the basis

With an existing quote, preserve that quote as a distinct commercial record.
A range described as bracketing its total must contain that total. The
quote-reconciliation tool checks this particular contract. A revised scope
may produce a different estimate; identify the change and compare it with
the original rather than silently moving the quoted value. If quoted figures do not
reconcile, preserve and label the discrepancy and request a corrected
authoritative source; never substitute a derived figure into a
client-facing deliverable.

Without a quote, derive the effort range from the scoped items. Apply
confirmed billing rates when available. A missing rate permits an effort
estimate, with cost still unresolved. The scope-project calculator
supports this local model without a CRM.

## Explain the width

If the source explicitly supplies a band, preserve its limits and cite that
source. Otherwise model the actual conditions affecting effort. A larger
data footprint or an additional interface may require different work;
state that work and its estimating basis.

Unknown information does not justify an arbitrary percentage. Keep an
unestimated dimension visible and identify the investigation needed to
bound it. Do not tighten a range merely to make an offer more attractive.
Use only the current project's confirmed capability assumptions and contingency policy.

## Preserve the pricing model

A fixed-fee quote has its confirmed amount and payment basis. A rough
estimate leading to such a quote is still a planning range. Keep their
status distinct.

For time and materials, explain the sourced rates and planned effort.
A ceiling or upfront percentage applies only when the agreement supplies
one. Do not create a change-order trigger from an estimating convention.
Preserve the actual scope and payment conditions.

## Name reopening conditions

Identify the specific condition that would change the estimate and the
work affected by it. A reopener names the revised estimating basis it
would require; it is not a contractual change-order trigger. A volume increase may require resizing an existing
item. A new platform may add a workstream. Their contractual consequences
come from the actual agreement; a rough estimate cannot establish them.

Keep the input revision with the range. Show the estimating basis at the
detail the buyer needs. Retain calculations and unresolved source conflicts
in the working record. A source conflict affecting the decision remains
visible in the delivered explanation too.
