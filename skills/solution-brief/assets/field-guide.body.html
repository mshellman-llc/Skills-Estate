2 · The charging model

Northgate charges by equipment class and membership tier

Northgate is a member-owned cooperative that rents shared farm equipment. Full members pay one daily rate. Associate members, who joined after the 2019 expansion and hold no equity, pay more. The FY26 schedule lists both columns for each of nine equipment classes.

There is no list price and no discounting. A reservation's cost is the class daily rate, times the tier, times the days out. Tom runs that lookup by hand against a printed sheet, then types the total into an invoice. He told us on 2 July that he has billed the wrong tier twice this season.

The proposed design uses the equipment class and member tier to select a rate. Equipment classes become products with a class attribute. The two tiers become two price books, resolved from the member record. Price book entries replace the manual lookup against the printed sheet.

6 · The conflict decision

Double booking: hard lock, soft warning, or an overbooking pool

Northgate overbooks on purpose. In harvest season two members routinely hold the same combine for the same week, and Tom reallocates by membership seniority the morning of. The requirements memo calls this "the way it works" and offers no rule. Choose how to handle overlapping reservations before designing the reservation records.

OptionMechanics and fitWatch
Hard lockScheduler refuses a second hold on a booked asset. This is the platform default and the simplest option to build. It prevents the deliberate overbooking Northgate uses today.Tom loses the flexibility he uses every harvest.
Soft warningThe second reservation saves with a conflict flag, and a queue shows Tom every clash to resolve. The queue records the overlaps Tom already manages. It requires a custom addition to standard scheduling.Needs a resolution screen and a seniority field.
Overbooking poolReservations attach to a class rather than a machine, and assignment happens at pickup. This is the largest development option. The reservation refers to an equipment class until a machine is assigned.Reshapes billing, since usage attaches to the machine.

Recommend the soft warning. It preserves the overbooking Northgate depends on, surfaces conflicts that are invisible today, and leaves the reservation attached to a machine so the telematics feed and the invoice still line up. Defer the pool option because it changes machine assignment and billing as well as conflict handling. Confirm the seniority rule with Reneé before committing either way.

8 · Against the in-house build

How to answer Dale Halverson

Halverson has supported Northgate for nine years and has proposed the full rebuild. Discuss a division of work: our team would configure scheduling and pricing; Halverson would retain the systems he manages. Confirm that split with Northgate and Halverson.

"I built the reservation database. I know this operation better than any consultant. Why pay you?"

Your experience with Northgate will inform the design. We propose leaving the maintenance database with you. Tier pricing must read the member record so the invoice uses that member’s assigned rate. Overbooking has to be expressed as a rule rather than as Tom's memory. The invoice needs structured usage data from the tracker to charge for actual use. The design must connect member pricing, reservation rules and usage data.

Agree which parts Halverson will own before estimating our work.

Ask them: which parts do you want to keep, and which should our team deliver? Agree the split in the design session.
"Two tiers and nine rates. That's a lookup table. Why is pricing hard?"

The rate lookup must use the correct member tier. Tom told us he billed the wrong tier twice this season, and 41 roster entries have no tier at all. Member-based pricing still requires the missing tiers to be filled in. The rate documents also disagree: the requirements memo says the associate multiplier is 1.5x and the rate schedule works out to 1.35x. Confirm the rate schedule before configuring either approach.

Ask them: which document governs the associate multiplier, and who can settle it this week? Confirm the governing rate before building the price books.
"We overbook on purpose. Your system will just block it."

The soft-warning option in section 6 allows overlapping reservations and records them in a queue. The queue records each overlap and how Tom resolves it. That history supports the member dispute reviews required by the memo.

Ask them: is reallocation seniority by join date, equity share, or something Tom carries in his head? We need it written down before it can be built.
"The tracker already reports hours. Isn't billing just reading that?"

We still need API credentials and a test device to inspect the feed. Then engine hours have to become billable days under a rule that does not exist yet: a machine idling in a yard reports hours too. That rule is Northgate's to set, which is why billing sits behind reservations.

Ask them: who holds the tracker contract, and can we get a test device plus credentials before the design session?
Commercial question

In this scenario, Halverson has proposed a lower price than our team can offer, and Reneé has seen it. Ask the commercial owner whether to pursue the design session and a smaller scope.
