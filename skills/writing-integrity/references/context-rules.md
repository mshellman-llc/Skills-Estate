# Content-integrity Rules

Read only the section that matches the current artifact. These are protected content constraints, not a personal voice profile or surface style guide. They do not authorize new claims or changes to meaning.

## Leadership and career writing

- Preserve senior leadership trajectory and ownership. Do not recast a senior leader as a generic individual contributor or default the story to billable delivery.
- Foreground technical go-to-market, pre-sales, program leadership, and concrete applied AI when supported by the source.
- Describe people leadership qualitatively unless the source supplies exact team size, reporting lines, or hiring authority.
- Keep the actual hiring case visible. Cut generic mission praise, recruiter-keyword stuffing, defensive disclaimers, and inflated claims.
- Distinguish base salary from total compensation when compensation appears.

## Commercial and delivery writing

- Preserve every price, hour estimate, role, assumption, exclusion, date, and qualifier exactly unless a source authorizes a change.
- Separate the included baseline from an incremental option. State each option's price, hours, proposed roles, scope, assumptions, exclusions, schedule effect, and approval status when the source provides them.
- Label estimates, client-reported facts, proposals, approvals, and open questions. Do not turn “proposed” into “committed” or “built locally” into “live.”
- Keep the full scope behind a concise executive view. Compression must not erase conditions or proof gaps.

## Presentations and visual artifacts

- Preserve approved text, figures, tables, order, and qualifiers when the request is visual-only.
- Do not mix facilitator notes into audience-facing copy unless requested.
- Keep each qualifier and source attached to the claim it governs.
- Do not let a shorter visual form change approval state, confidence, ownership, or scope.

## Reflective or personal writing

- Preserve uncertainty, context, self-correction, and the writer's active process of figuring something out.
- Use concrete scenes or examples when the source supplies them. Do not invent a personal story.
- Do not force a binary position, polished thesis, or motivational ending onto a piece that is still exploring.
- Keep a real rough edge when it carries personality or emotional truth.

## Sensitive internal or high-stakes personal writing

- Lead with the decision, boundary, or requested action.
- Include only the sensitive detail needed for the recipient to act.
- Bound transition help, exceptions, or support by owner, scope, and end point.
- Treat “complete,” “approved,” “remaining,” and similar handoff claims as facts that need records. Label them unverified when proof is missing.
- Make the next action concrete without creating an obligation the writer did not authorize.

## Organizational or regulated voice

- Do not apply a personal voice over legal, contractual, regulatory, or approved organizational language.
- Preserve defined terms and mandated disclosures verbatim.
- When a brand system applies, let its specific rules govern vocabulary and presentation after facts and legal meaning are protected.
- If writing for another person, preserve that person's supplied voice rather than imitating the user.
