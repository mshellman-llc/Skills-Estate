# Surface Rules

Use only the section for the current output. The same fact should survive every surface, but the form should not. A website, email, and deck that share one template-shaped cadence will all feel generated even when every sentence is grammatical.

## Website and product UI

### Tells

- A hero makes a universal promise without naming the user, job, mechanism, or limit.
- Every section repeats the same centered heading, short paragraph, and equal card grid.
- Gradients, glows, glass panels, pills, icons, and large rounded cards provide identity in place of information hierarchy.
- Feature cards restate labels instead of showing a workflow, decision, comparison, or result.
- Statistics, testimonials, customer names, status data, or dashboard values are invented to make the interface look complete.
- Calls to action use vague labels such as “Get started” when the next action is more specific.
- Controls look interactive but do nothing, or the interface hides its primary action behind decorative motion.
- UI copy describes the system in marketing language instead of naming the object, state, problem, and recovery action.

### Repairs

- Start with the user's actual job and the primary action the page enables.
- Let the information determine the layout. Use cards for independent objects, a sequence for steps, a table for comparison, and prose when the point is simply prose.
- Replace decorative variety with a small visual system: intentional type hierarchy, spacing, accessible contrast, and real states.
- Use real data from the supplied source or label synthetic demo data plainly. Never fabricate proof.
- Give buttons and links action-specific labels. Error and empty states name what happened and how to recover.
- Preserve application behavior. This skill may change human-facing copy and presentation, not silently redesign business logic.

### Proof

- Inspect the rendered page at desktop and mobile widths, not only the source.
- Exercise every visible primary action and confirm that labels match behavior.
- Check light and dark themes when both exist, keyboard focus, contrast, alt text, overflow, and empty or error states.
- Tie claims, figures, and examples back to the supplied source. For Aleysian work, `brand-renderer` owns the stricter brand and publication gate.

## Email and messages

### Tells

- A ceremonial greeting or generic warmth delays the reason for writing.
- Context arrives before the decision, correction, request, or current condition.
- Three parallel benefit sentences or a polished mini-essay replaces one concrete ask.
- A boundary is softened until the recipient cannot tell what is in, out, approved, or needed.
- The ending recaps the email or offers generic help instead of naming the next action.

### Repairs

- Open on the fact, decision, question, or requested outcome.
- Use specific warmth only when the source supports the relationship detail.
- Separate discrete questions when the recipient must answer them independently.
- Name the owner, date, decision, or option when the source provides one.
- Stop when the content ends. Do not add a chatbot closer.

### Proof

- Read it aloud as if sending it to the named recipient.
- Verify recipients, names, dates, money, scope, and attachments against the source.
- For consequential messages, separate confirmed facts, assumptions, open checks, and the exact requested action.

## Slide decks

### Tells

- Every slide uses the same title, subtitle, and three-card grid regardless of its communication job.
- Labels such as “Overview,” “Key takeaways,” or “Our approach” occupy the headline without making a claim.
- Support copy becomes clipped fragments or slogan-like lines in an attempt to sound punchy.
- The deck repeats one fact across several slides, rotates synonyms, or uses a colon reveal on every page.
- Sources and qualifiers are tiny, detached, or removed while the claim becomes more prominent.
- Decorative diagrams or dashboard mockups imply relationships the evidence does not support.

### Repairs

- Give each slide one communication job: establish a fact, compare options, explain a sequence, show evidence, or ask for a decision.
- Write a headline that carries the point. Use complete support sentences unless a true label or datum is clearer.
- Choose composition from the content. A timeline, comparison, hierarchy, table, quote, or full-bleed statement should not be forced into the same grid.
- Keep each claim beside its source, qualifier, and units. Give a fact one authoritative home.
- Vary visual rhythm through content structure, not random decoration or chopped prose.

### Proof

- Render every slide to images or PDF and inspect the actual views for clipping, density, hierarchy, and detached qualifiers.
- Extract the deck text and compare names, figures, and protected wording with the approved source.
- Play the deck in order. The narrative should advance rather than restate itself.
- For Aleysian work, `brand-renderer` owns tokens, assets, accessibility, parity, and release evidence.

## Documents, reports, and proposals

### Tells

- The document follows a generic template even when sections do not serve the reader's decision.
- Every section has the same length, internal pattern, and recap.
- An executive summary repeats the body without making the decision or tradeoff easier to see.
- Bullets, bold labels, and headings break prose into decorated fragments.
- Generic confidence language obscures which claims are confirmed, estimated, reported, proposed, or unknown.

### Repairs

- Organize around the reader's decisions, questions, and evidence needs.
- Use prose, lists, tables, and visuals according to the information relationship.
- Let sections run to their natural length and delete slots the content does not need.
- Separate fact, interpretation, recommendation, assumption, and open decision.
- Preserve the full scope and proof behind a concise top layer.

### Proof

- Reopen or render the saved format and inspect page breaks, tables, callouts, footnotes, and reading order.
- Reconcile every figure and qualifier with the source.
- Confirm that the summary can be traced to the body and does not overstate it.

## Boundary

This skill evaluates human-facing language, information shape, and visible presentation. It does not replace accessibility engineering, functional testing, code review, application security review, or a brand-specific renderer. Route those concerns to their owners and bring their findings back into the final proof.
